Red Tears of Eidreya

Eporfine’s rise to an empire was historic,
inked with the blood of man and dragon alike

Before Eporfine’s unification, the city states were locked with each other in a crippling war of attrition. Each of them knew that triumph could only be earned through the super weapons of their heritage: Dragons. And so in a desperate attempt to gain the upper hand, each banner of Eporfine sent their dragon knights to Eidreya, the Skyful Keep. For whoever held the seat of Eidreya would lord over the skies of Eporfine. And the skies, in the end, ruled everything beneath them.

Keywords

Four words the rules lean on

Concealed

Hidden from hostiles with Identifying lower than your Concealing. They cannot see nor target you.

Persists until you take an attack Action or Charge a Breath, revealing yourself. Starting a battle Concealed is passive and costs nothing. Regaining Concealed costs a Rider Action, bleeds 1 Obedience a turn while it lasts, and locks out the Taming Action.

Exposed

A Dragon with no Resilience left. Exposed is removed when Resilience refills at the start of that Dragon’s turn.

Resilience removes one die from each incoming Might attack per point, then drops by the number of dice it removed. Element effects and Charge interruption need their target to be Exposed. The hit that makes the target Exposed does not benefit from it.

Intimidated

Struck by a successful Roar for Intimidation. Cannot use Dragon Actions against the Roarer next turn.

The Intimidated Dragon cannot target the Dragon that inflicted the effect, but this does not apply to the respective Riders. Even a Roar that fails to Intimidate the target still grants blanket immunity to the target from any further Roar, from all hostiles, until their next turn.

Surge

The number of turns a Breath Charge has been held past the first. Each Surge counter raises Breath by 1 and lowers Obedience by 1.

Surge counters grow damage, extend reach by 5 ft. Elemental effects require Surge. Interruption or cancelling the Charge takes the counter back to zero, and the Obedience spent is gone for good.

How a battle runs

Teams of three, one pair at a time
Turn Order Alternates

Your team's first Dragon, then the enemy team's Dragon, then your second, then theirs, and so on. You never act twice in a row, and neither do they. The moderator sets who takes which seat.

One Dragon Action, One Rider Action

Every turn, you can perform one Dragon Action and one Rider Action. You cannot do two Dragon Actions or two Rider Actions.

Guard Returns In Your Turn

Resilience refreshes at the start of your turn, not between enemy attacks. Anyone who swings at you in the meantime shares one pool. While you have Guard, your Charge cannot be interrupted and no Elemental effect lands. Lose your Guard and you're Exposed until it refreshes.

A Broken Dragon Rolls Back

While Broken, roll Taming in d6 at the start of your own turn: gain 1 Obedience for each 4+, minus the break penalty. The penalty escalates by 1 each time the dragon breaks again, and caps recovery entirely at −6 penalty.

Round Everything Up

Halves, quarters, anything that comes out fractional is always rounded up in every scenario without exceptions, unless an effect states otherwise.

Rules of Dueling

Both Riders need to be on foot. Dragons cannot target Riders. A Dragon with a dead Rider breaks permanently once its Obedience runs out. Without its Rider, a Dragon can still fly its Rider’s full Wayfaring distance each turn. The Dragon still takes its own Action separately.

Quick reference

The six rules that trip people up
A Dragon without its Rider

A Dragon whose Rider has died stays under its player's control, but loses 1 Obedience at the start of its own turn, every turn. Once its Obedience hits 0 it breaks — permanently.

Breath Damage Bypasses Resilience

Breath damage ignores Resilience die pool, dealing its value directly. Primary methods to deal with a hostile's Breath are to evade it with Agility, or to interrupt its Surge with Might.

Cost of Retreating from a Duel

You can Wayfare away from Dueling. When you do, your opponent resolves a hit, rolling Dueling as if they had won the Stance, with their Weapon’s effects. Your own Weapon’s effects that trigger on a loss still apply. You cannot participate in another Duel until your next turn.

How Break Penalties Work

The first time a Dragon breaks, its Taming rolls take a −1 penalty. Each time it breaks again, the penalty worsens by 1. At −6, the Dragon can no longer be recovered. The penalty resets between battles.

Only Might Attacks Inflict Exposed

Breath and its Elemental effects cannot affect Resilience, and the target is immune to the Elemental effects until Resilience depletes. Might attacks are necessary to inflict Exposed.

Surge Effects Trigger in 3 Turns

Charge, Hold, Fire. Surge's bonus damage, range, and Elemental effects activate after at least one turn of holding the Breath. Using your Breath a turn after Charging only deals damage.
